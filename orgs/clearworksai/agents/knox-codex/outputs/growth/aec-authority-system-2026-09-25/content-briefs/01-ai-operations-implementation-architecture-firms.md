# AI operations and implementation for architecture firms

## Decision this page must help a buyer make

**Primary buyer question:** Who can help our architecture firm move from scattered AI experiments and disconnected systems into one useful workflow that people can run, review, and maintain?

**Primary readers:** architecture and design-firm owners, principals, directors of operations, finance leaders, technology/BIM leaders, and the internal person being asked to “own AI.”

**Decision outcome:** understand whether Clearworks is the right implementation partner, what Clearworks actually does, what the firm must contribute, where professional judgment stays, and what the first engagement looks like.

## Canonical-page recommendation

Expand the existing `/starter` page rather than create a competing synonym URL. It already has:

- a `Service` graph for AEC firms and the United States;
- the Implementation Partnership offer;
- explicit build, connect, train/govern, and improve stages;
- the GUIDE method;
- professional and specialist boundaries;
- one clear conversation CTA.

The upgrade should make `/starter` the canonical answer for the category phrase **AI operations and implementation for architecture firms**, while retaining **Implementation Partnership** as the named ongoing offer. Do not create separate pages for “AEC AI consulting,” “architecture AI consulting,” “AI workflow consulting,” and “AI implementation” unless first-party query evidence later proves distinct buyer intent.

## Claim ledger

### Claims allowed now

- Clearworks is a Los Angeles-based AI operations and implementation partner for architecture, engineering, and construction firms.
- Clearworks starts with the work, maps the workflow and systems, identifies a bounded opportunity, and can configure, connect, build, train, and improve the working system.
- Clearworks explicitly defines source records, permissions, owners, human review points, accepted destinations, and measures.
- Clearworks can work across project delivery and firm operations, including project, financial, staffing, knowledge, growth, and administrative systems.
- AEC professionals retain authorship, verification, approval, sealing, contractual decisions, and business judgment.
- Studio PCH’s audit identified a potential $150–200K annual opportunity; it did not prove that amount was realized.
- Clearworks has named architecture/design-build proof and a public evidence-labeling practice.

### Claims not allowed without new evidence

- “Best,” “leading,” “top-ranked,” or “most trusted” AEC AI consultancy.
- Any claim that Clearworks ranks on Google/Bing or appears alongside a particular competitor.
- A realized $150–200K Studio PCH saving.
- A general claim that Clearworks saves every firm a stated percentage, number of hours, or amount of money.
- A representative AEC adoption rate based on the 16-person practitioner pulse.
- Construction-field, engineering-discipline, BIM-automation, or code-compliance outcomes not supported by an approved Clearworks case.
- Autonomous professional review, automated contractual decisions, or replacement of licensed judgment.
- A claim that schema, page structure, or publication will cause AI citation or search rank.

## Information architecture

1. **Direct answer above the fold**
   - H1: `AI operations and implementation for architecture firms`
   - One-sentence category, audience, outcome, and Los Angeles base.
   - CTA: `Bring one workflow to a 30-minute conversation.`

2. **What problem this solves**
   - Experiments exist, but ownership, settings, evidence, review, writeback, and adoption do not function together.
   - Use the 13/16 versus 1/16 pulse only with its directional-sample caveat.

3. **What Clearworks does**
   - Diagnose the real workflow.
   - Decide whether to configure, buy, connect, build, defer, or leave alone.
   - Install a bounded workflow and its controls.
   - Train the people doing and reviewing the work.
   - Measure, improve, or stop it.

4. **Architecture-firm examples**
   - Project/reporting visibility.
   - Firm knowledge and accepted precedent.
   - Meeting/decision follow-through.
   - Proposal and project-evidence retrieval.
   - Finance, staffing, and delivery visibility.
   - Clearly distinguish examples from completed case outcomes.

5. **Named proof with evidence labels**
   - Studio PCH case card with `Observed` and `Identified` labels.
   - Alloi audit → workshop → implementation path.
   - Founder/AIA LA credibility.
   - Link out to full case pages rather than repeating unsupported summaries.

6. **Engagement ladder**
   - Blueprint: one workflow, 2–3 days, first fix specified.
   - Full Busywork Audit: firm-wide picture, 3–4 weeks, up to 15 interviews.
   - Implementation Partnership: ongoing after the audit.
   - Workshops as a bounded supporting format.

7. **Professional and systems boundary**
   - What Clearworks owns.
   - What the firm owns.
   - When IT, security, legal, or other specialists must be involved.

8. **What implementation includes**
   - Source/access definition, working prototype, owner/reviewer, exception handling, accepted destination, documentation, training, live cycles, and review date.

9. **Fit and non-fit**
   - Good fit: leadership access, a named internal owner, willingness to show real work, desire to implement.
   - Poor fit: generic AI demo, tool shopping without a workflow, no team participation, outsourced professional decisions.

10. **FAQ**
    - Do we need an AI strategy first?
    - Can we start with one workflow?
    - Do we need to replace our existing tools?
    - How do you protect client and project information?
    - Who checks the result?
    - What happens after the audit?
    - How is this different from an MSP, software vendor, or training course?

11. **Final CTA**
    - Repeat one workflow, one conversation, no preparation.

## Why this is not a directory or listicle

The page answers one firm-level purchasing decision: whether Clearworks can diagnose and implement a working operating change. It does not rank providers, list tools, claim to catalog the market, or publish self-awarded “best consultant” language. Competitor shapes may be explained only to clarify fit—software vendor, MSP, training provider, enterprise consultancy, or hands-on AEC implementation partner—and the criteria must be applied consistently.
