# The AEC Busywork Audit

## Decision this page must help a buyer make

**Primary buyer question:** We know work is taking too long and too much depends on a few people, but we do not know which problem to fix first. What will a Busywork Audit show us, what evidence will it use, and what will we be able to decide afterward?

**Primary readers:** AEC owners, principals, directors of operations, finance leaders, practice leaders, and technology leaders considering an audit, assessment, or roadmap engagement.

**Decision outcome:** understand whether the firm needs a one-workflow Blueprint, a firm-wide Busywork Audit, or a lighter workshop; know what the audit does and does not deliver; and feel confident that modeled opportunity will not be misrepresented as realized savings.

## Canonical-page recommendation

Create one dedicated `/busywork-audit` page. The current offer lives mainly inside homepage sections and anchors, which makes its method, proof, fit, and evidence boundaries difficult to cite or evaluate as one canonical object.

Do not create separate pages for “AI workflow audit,” “AEC workflow audit,” “architecture firm assessment,” “AI readiness assessment,” and “operational audit.” Those phrases can be answered as sections or FAQs on the same page unless first-party data later demonstrates distinct intent.

## Claim ledger

### Claims allowed now

- The Full Busywork Audit maps how work actually moves across people, systems, handoffs, and review points.
- The audit can use interviews, surveys, workflow artifacts, project files, systems inventories, and time/value assumptions to identify and prioritize opportunities.
- The current offer is described as 3–4 weeks and up to 15 interviews, subject to final commercial confirmation.
- The output is a shared picture of friction, directional opportunity, dependencies, risks, and next moves leadership can evaluate.
- Recommendations may include documentation, redesign, configuration, connection, automation, training, governance, or leaving the work alone.
- Studio PCH’s audit identified $150–200K in potential annual opportunity and observed 30% rework plus 20 weekly hours of manual finance work.
- The audit is not itself proof that the identified value will be realized.

### Claims not allowed without new evidence

- The audit “saves” every firm a stated amount or percentage.
- Studio PCH realized $150–200K in savings.
- The audit is statistically representative of architecture firms or the broader AEC market.
- Every audit includes the same number of interviews, deliverables, or calendar duration regardless of scope.
- Clearworks has a proprietary score proven to predict ROI.
- Clearworks replaces accounting, legal, cybersecurity, professional, or management judgment.
- The page ranks on Google/Bing or has demonstrated coexistence with another provider.
- The page will be cited by AI systems because it includes schema, statistics, or FAQs.

## Proposed direct answer

> A Clearworks Busywork Audit gives an AEC leadership team a shared, evidence-backed view of where recurring work, handoffs, disconnected systems, and key-person dependencies consume time or create risk. It does not promise that every identified hour or dollar will be recovered. It shows what is happening, what may be worth changing, what the change would require, and what to address first.

## Information architecture

1. **Hero: make the hidden work visible**
   - H1: `Find the work your firm keeps rebuilding.`
   - Category line: `A Busywork Audit for architecture, engineering, and construction firms.`
   - Explain 3–4 weeks/up to 15 interviews only after commercial confirmation.
   - CTA: `Bring the work that keeps coming back to a 30-minute conversation.`

2. **When an audit is the right first step**
   - Repeated reporting or reconciliation.
   - Too much work routed through a few people.
   - Systems that hold conflicting versions of state.
   - AI experiments without ownership or a sequence.
   - Leadership sees symptoms but not the whole operating pattern.

3. **Blueprint, Audit, Workshop, or Pilot?**
   - Comparison table with purpose, scope, output, internal effort, and next condition.
   - Blueprint: one workflow.
   - Audit: firm-wide evidence and sequence.
   - Workshop: bounded working session or enablement.
   - Pilot/Implementation Partnership: live change.

4. **What evidence the audit uses**
   - Interviews and short surveys.
   - Current workflows and handoffs.
   - Project and business artifacts.
   - Systems and source-of-truth review.
   - Staff time and cost assumptions.
   - Security, governance, ownership, and readiness constraints.
   - State which artifacts are optional, permissioned, anonymized, or excluded.

5. **How the analysis works, without giving away the proprietary engine**
   - Trace the work.
   - Separate unique burdens from overlapping descriptions.
   - Label observed facts, staff estimates, modeled opportunities, and hypotheses.
   - Rank by value, readiness, risk, dependencies, and ability to own the result.

6. **What the team receives**
   - Operating picture and pain atlas.
   - Workflow and system map.
   - Opportunity portfolio with evidence and confidence labels.
   - Recommended sequence and first bounded move.
   - Adoption/security/governance constraints.
   - Leadership readout and roadmap.
   - Confirm exact artifact names before publication.

7. **Named architecture/design proof**
   - Studio PCH evidence table.
   - Alloi audit-to-implementation mini-path if approved.
   - Link to full case study.

8. **Evidence labels**
   - Observed.
   - Staff-estimated.
   - Identified/modelled.
   - Implemented.
   - Client-reported.
   - Realized and verified, only when the evidence exists.

9. **What happens next**
   - Use the roadmap internally.
   - Run a bounded workshop or build.
   - Continue through the Implementation Partnership.
   - Stop if the evidence does not justify implementation.

10. **FAQ**
    - Is this an AI readiness assessment?
    - Do we have to share client files?
    - How much staff time does it require?
    - How are opportunities calculated?
    - What if the answer is “do not automate this”?
    - Can we begin with one workflow?
    - What does implementation cost after the audit?

11. **Final CTA**
    - `Bring the work that keeps coming back to a 30-minute conversation.`
    - Secondary: `Start with one workflow instead` → Blueprint.
