# Upgrade plan for three existing architecture-firm authority pages

## Governing rule

Preserve all three canonical URLs. Each page already answers a distinct buyer decision:

1. **Where to start:** choose the first bounded workflow and test.
2. **Which tools to standardize:** choose the smallest maintainable environment and decide whether to use, configure, buy, connect, build, defer, or leave alone.
3. **Modern AEC Firm Playbook:** understand which operating capability and transition needs attention as the firm grows.

The upgrade should strengthen evidence, decision usefulness, internal-link architecture, and CTA fit without turning the pages into overlapping “AI for architecture firms” articles.

## Evidence boundary across all three pages

- A generic web-discovery tool returned these Clearworks pages for precise questions or the navigational playbook phrase. That ordering is not Google/Bing rank evidence.
- On September 26, Josh manually searched Google for `where should an architecture firm start with AI` and did not see Clearworks in his results. This bounded observation invalidates any claim that the research proved a Google win, but it does not prove universal absence.
- Google organic position, Bing position, local-pack presence, and coexistence with AEC Hub or AI in AEC remain unverified.
- Preserve the current URLs, establish the Search Console baselines, and diagnose before rewriting solely for a discovery sample.

# Page A — Where should an architecture firm start with AI?

## Buyer question

What is the smallest useful first AI move for our architecture firm, how do we choose it, and what must be true before we scale?

## What the live page already does well

- Gives a direct, task-first answer.
- Starts with recurring work rather than an AI strategy deck.
- Names a trigger, desired help, qualified reviewer, accepted destination, and workflow owner.
- Gives four concrete candidates: meeting-to-action, model-to-visualization, drawing review, and intake-to-follow-up.
- Establishes account, data, connection, and approval rules.
- Ends with a keep/change/stop review rather than automatic scaling.
- Uses grounded Clearworks voice and avoids a tool catalog.

## Current gaps

- No visible evidence or source section.
- No explicit explanation of why the recommendation differs by Solo/5/15/50/150 firm stage.
- No example showing the difference between a candidate workflow and an accepted result.
- No explicit first-30/60/90-day sequence.
- No direct link to the service path most appropriate after the reader chooses a workflow.
- No measurement worksheet or baseline example.
- The page does not state that the generic discovery result is not rank evidence; that statement belongs in internal reporting, not necessarily page copy, but measurement must remain bounded.

## Exact evidence available

| Evidence | Use on page | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Clearworks practitioner pulse: 13/16 respondents experimenting or deployed in at least one workflow; only one cross-team workflow. | Explain why a bounded operating test matters more than more isolated experimentation. | Directional sample only. |
| Studio PCH: knowledge in people, 30% rework observed, 20 weekly manual finance hours observed. | Example of starting from operating friction rather than a tool. | Named case labels remain intact. |
| Alloi live workshop automated a recurring workflow costing approximately 30 hours/month of staff time, with people checking the result. | Concrete bounded implementation example if client approval covers this placement. | Do not infer realized savings or generalize. |
| Workflow Atlas: 91 examples, each framed as trigger → source → AI action → human gate → record → measure. | Give readers a structured way to choose a candidate. | The Atlas maps possibility, not suitability for every firm. |
| AIA AI Firm Toolkit and current Clearworks field report. | Professional-review, ownership, and adoption context. | Cite directly; do not write a competing policy template. |

## Claims allowed

- Start with one recurring workflow whose beginning, end, reviewer, and accepted destination can be named.
- A first test should have an owner, data boundary, review point, and scheduled decision to keep, change, or stop.
- Small firms should not copy an enterprise AI lab; the implementation shape depends on systems, roles, risk, and capacity.
- Clearworks can help map one workflow through a Blueprint or the broader operating picture through a Busywork Audit.

## Claims not allowed

- This page currently ranks for its question.
- This is the universally best sequence for all architecture firms.
- Any example will save a stated number of hours without an approved baseline.
- One successful workflow proves the whole firm is AI-ready.
- A specific tool is “best” without context, method, and date.

## Likely query/prompt families

- where should an architecture firm start with AI
- architecture firm first AI workflow
- first 30 days of AI for an architecture firm
- architecture firm AI first 90 days
- best first AI use case for a small architecture firm
- how to choose an AI pilot for an architecture firm
- workshop versus audit versus pilot for AEC AI
- how to keep an architecture AI pilot safe

Verified volume for the exact first-step phrases is unknown or zero reported. The page is justified by recurring buyer questions and its place in the authority journey, not a volume forecast.

## Recommended information architecture

1. Preserve the direct answer.
2. Add a five-question “good first workflow” screen:
   - frequent;
   - bounded;
   - painful enough to matter;
   - reviewable by a named person;
   - accepted result has a clear home.
3. Add “do not start here” counterexamples: consequential professional decisions, inconsistent source records, rare one-off work, or no internal owner.
4. Add a 30/60/90-day sequence:
   - 0–30: observe and baseline;
   - 31–60: run a bounded version on real records;
   - 61–90: compare evidence and keep/revise/stop.
5. Add size-specific notes from Solo/5/15/50/150 without duplicating the full playbook.
6. Add one labeled case/example.
7. Add a printable first-workflow worksheet.
8. Add links to Workflow Atlas, Tool Standardization, Modern AEC Firm, Busywork Audit, and `/starter`.
9. Add CTA based on uncertainty: `Bring one workflow. We will help you decide whether it is worth changing.`

## Proof still needed

- One approved bounded-workflow case with baseline, reviewer, accepted destination, and four-cycle result.
- One Josh quote about why starting with a tool distorts the work.
- A redacted workflow-selection worksheet.
- First-party Search Console and engagement baseline for the existing URL.

## CTA

**Primary:** `Bring one recurring workflow.`

**Secondary:** `See 91 workflow examples` to the Atlas.

Do not lead with a generic newsletter or tool download.

# Page B — Which AI tools should an architecture firm standardize?

## Buyer question

Which shared AI environment can our firm actually govern, support, and use on real work—and when should we configure, buy, connect, build, defer, or leave the work alone?

## What the live page already does well

- Starts with a small tool set rather than “more tools.”
- Recommends one general assistant initially and a specialist only for a named job.
- Distinguishes the model from the application and its controls/connections.
- Gives six practical evaluation questions.
- Defines use what you have, buy, connect, and build.
- Names account ownership, privacy settings, onboarding/offboarding, role-based training, and renewal.
- Ends with maintainability rather than capability hype.

## Current gaps

- The decision model should include **configure**, **defer**, and **leave alone** explicitly.
- No dated methodology or update policy for the named ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude examples.
- No comparison worksheet with authoritative record, permissions, review, exception rate, writeback, maintenance owner, reversibility, and exit plan.
- No current cost/price assumptions, clearly dated and separated from durable principles.
- No example where the right answer was not to buy or build.
- No sources or case proof.
- No explicit relationship to the Implementation Partnership or Busywork Audit.

## Exact evidence available

| Evidence | Use on page | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `AI tools for architects` had 140 reported US monthly searches; `architecture AI tools` had 110. | Establish a real broad tool-discovery audience. | Those are list-heavy product queries, not proof of service intent. |
| `software for architects` had 5,400 reported searches; `architecture project management software` had 720. | Explain why product-shopping traffic is large but often wrong for Clearworks. | Do not target these as vanity head terms. |
| Exact phrases such as `AI tools for architecture firms`, `architecture firm software`, and `architecture firm technology stack` returned zero reported. | Treat firm-standardization long-tails as high-fit but unproven demand. | “0 reported,” not no searches. |
| A generic discovery query returned this page, AEC Hub, and AIA for the precise standardization question. | Preserve and measure the existing asset. | Not Google/Bing rank or coexistence evidence. |
| Modern AEC Firm Technology & AI pillar says each system needs a job; accounts, permissions, owners, human review, writeback, integration, and adoption change with firm size. | Add operating context without recreating the full 7×5 matrix. | Clearworks operating model, not an industry standard. |
| Strategy ladder defines build, buy, configure, connect, leave alone, and defer. | Complete the public decision framework. | Publish the principle, protect proprietary firm-specific scoring. |

## Claims allowed

- Architecture firms usually need a smaller, owned AI environment rather than a long product list.
- Product choice depends on the job, information, permissions, output authority, review, fit with existing systems, cost, owner, and exit path.
- General assistants and specialist tools solve different jobs.
- A successful demo does not establish maintainability, adoption, or trusted writeback.
- Clearworks can help a firm evaluate whether to configure, buy, connect, build, defer, or leave a workflow alone.

## Claims not allowed

- ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude is universally best.
- The page’s current product names, prices, or features are permanent.
- Clearworks has verified every tool in a large directory.
- The page ranks for the broad tool terms.
- Tool standardization alone creates adoption or ROI.
- Product logos imply partnership, endorsement, or compatibility.

## Likely query/prompt families

- which AI tools should an architecture firm standardize
- AI tools for architects
- architecture AI tools
- best AI platform for a small architecture firm
- ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini for architecture firms
- should an architecture firm buy another AI tool
- build vs buy vs connect AI for AEC
- what should an architecture firm stop buying
- architecture firm AI governance and account ownership
- how to evaluate an AI tool using real architecture work

## Recommended information architecture

1. Preserve the current short answer.
2. Add a dated context block: named products are examples, not permanent winners.
3. Expand the choice model to:
   - use/configure what you have;
   - buy;
   - connect;
   - build;
   - defer;
   - leave alone.
4. Add a decision table with:
   - job;
   - source data;
   - authoritative record;
   - actions allowed;
   - human decision;
   - exception rate;
   - integration/writeback;
   - owner/support;
   - cost of change;
   - reversibility/exit.
5. Add role examples: project architect, operations, finance, marketing/BD, BIM/technology.
6. Add a “when not to standardize” section.
7. Add one approved real decision example for each of configure/connect/buy/build/leave alone as evidence becomes available.
8. Add downloadable decision worksheet.
9. Link to first-workflow page, Modern AEC Firm, Workflow Atlas, Busywork Audit, and Implementation Partnership.
10. CTA: `Bring the tool list and the workflow it is supposed to improve.`

## Proof still needed

- Current dated comparison of general assistants under a written test method, if product comparison remains on the page.
- At least two real decision examples, including one “do not buy/build” outcome.
- A template showing permissions, ownership, review, and exit planning.
- Confirmation that public product/price facts can be maintained quarterly.
- Search Console baseline for the existing URL.

## CTA

**Primary:** `Bring the tool list and the workflow it is supposed to improve.`

**Secondary:** downloadable build/buy/configure/connect/defer worksheet.

# Page C — Modern AEC Firm Playbook

## Buyer question

As our firm grows, which operating capability is failing first, what foundation did we skip, and what should we strengthen next without copying the systems of a much larger firm?

## What the live page already does well

- Defines seven connected pillars across five size archetypes.
- Explicitly says headcount is a proxy and the size bands are not official industry categories.
- Gives a 35-cell 7×5 matrix with concrete operating moves.
- Explains the distinct constraint at Solo, 5, 15, 50, and 150 people.
- Provides a five-stage modernization sequence.
- Provides a seven-question scorecard.
- Connects the page to the larger Clearworks resource universe.
- Includes first-party and institutional sources plus `TechArticle` and `ItemList` structure.
- Keeps software/tool choices out of the durable framework.

## Current gaps

- The five size bands need a visible methodology note explaining that they are planning archetypes derived from Clearworks synthesis, not survey categories.
- The page needs a stronger transition-oriented navigation: “what changes from 5 to 15,” not only static size columns.
- The public scorecard asks good questions but does not produce a durable output or route the reader to the right next resource.
- The free/public boundary versus the forthcoming book/community should be clearer.
- The page lacks original benchmark data by size; it must not imply that the cells are measured prevalence.
- The universe section should link to canonical commercial pages once the service and Busywork Audit pages exist.
- The downloadable discussion kit needs defined measurement and consent handling.

## Exact evidence available

| Evidence | Use on page | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| AIA firm research cited on the page found different day-to-day AI use by firm size. | Show that operating context differs by scale. | The Clearworks five bands are not AIA categories. |
| Clearworks field evidence includes smaller firms, transition pressures, and recurring ownership/system gaps. | Add labeled field notes. | Not representative prevalence by size. |
| The strategy ladder documents the book’s durable differentiator: size-first transitions across seven whole-firm capabilities. | Strengthen the canonical hub role. | This is Clearworks IP/positioning, not a validated market category. |
| Exact size-query demand is unverified or zero reported; 50/150 modifiers did not create distinct discovery samples. | Keep one canonical hub rather than thin size pages. | Split only after first-party evidence. |
| The generic discovery tool returned the page for its navigational phrase. | Protect the URL and measure it. | Not Google/Bing rank ownership. |

## Claims allowed

- The seven pillars and five size bands are Clearworks operating models.
- Headcount is a planning lens, not a maturity score.
- A useful way to read the model is one size backward for skipped foundations and one forward for likely next pressure.
- Workflows, technology, knowledge, finance, leadership, positioning, and business development affect one another.
- Firms at different scales should not maintain the same operating architecture.

## Claims not allowed

- The five sizes are industry-standard categories.
- Every firm hits the same problem at the same headcount.
- The scorecard is statistically validated or predicts performance.
- The page ranks for “Modern AEC Firm,” owns the term, or coexists with specific competitors on Google/Bing.
- A named technology stack is universally correct for a size band.
- Clearworks benchmark results by size until the sample and methodology support them.

## Likely query/prompt families

- Modern AEC Firm Playbook
- technology stack for a 5-person architecture firm
- technology stack for a 15-person architecture firm
- what changes when an architecture firm grows from 5 to 15 people
- architecture firm operating model
- architecture firm systems and processes
- architecture firm capacity, finance, workflow, and growth systems
- solo architecture firm minimum viable operating stack
- first AI workflow by architecture-firm size
- how should a 50-person architecture firm manage AI and technology

Demand for the size-transition framework remains unknown. Broader software and project-management terms have demand but are not the hub’s intended buyer decision.

## Recommended information architecture

1. Preserve the current hero, seven pillars, five sizes, matrix, sequence, scorecard, and source list.
2. Add `Choose your path` navigation:
   - by current firm size;
   - by transition;
   - by operating problem/pillar.
3. Add transition summaries:
   - Solo → 5;
   - 5 → 15;
   - 15 → 50;
   - 50 → 150.
4. Add “what not to build yet” in each transition.
5. Add visible methodology/evidence note near the matrix.
6. Add selected public field observations with explicit evidence classes.
7. Turn the seven-question scorecard into a useful output that points to one resource, not a maturity grade.
8. Add canonical links to service, Busywork Audit, project reporting, Workflow Atlas, State of AI, first-workflow, tool-standardization, FAQ, and glossary.
9. Clarify the value ladder:
   - public hub = organizing map and selected evidence;
   - book = full durable synthesis, tradeoffs, and cases;
   - community = application and updates;
   - audit/implementation = firm-specific evidence and change.
10. CTA: `Find the operating connection holding your firm back.`

## Proof still needed

- A published methodology note for the seven pillars and five archetypes.
- Permissioned and anonymized transition observations by firm size.
- Sufficient benchmark sample before publishing any prevalence table.
- Scorecard response schema, privacy boundary, and outcome-routing logic.
- One approved quote from an owner at each available transition; absence should remain visible rather than filled with composites.
- Search Console baseline for the canonical URL.

## CTA

**Primary:** `Use the seven-question scorecard.`

**Commercial follow-on:** `Bring the weak connection to a conversation` or `See the Busywork Audit.`

The public answer must remain useful without downloading the book or submitting a form.

- Export 28 days of Search Console page/query data for all three URLs.
- Export the dedicated Generative AI performance view for these pages if available.
- Preserve existing page content and screenshot/raw HTML.
- Record current internal-link sources and crawl/indexation state.

## After each staged upgrade

- Publish one page at a time so changes are interpretable.
- Confirm canonical selection, indexation, crawlability, structured-data validity, and rendered content.
- Monitor Search Console queries/impressions/clicks/average position by page.
- Track generative-answer mention, citation, accurate-category description, and citation support separately.
- Track internal-entry source, engagement, download/use, CTA, accepted lead, booking, and qualified opportunity separately.
- Preserve originating awareness and subsequent site validation as different attribution fields.

## Decision rules

- No rank or coexistence claims without observed, dated, location/personalization-controlled evidence.
- Do not split size/tool/workflow variants into new URLs based only on a keyword list.
- A rise in impressions without qualified engagement is not sufficient evidence to expand.
- A qualified conversation can justify a low-traffic page, but one anecdote does not establish demand.
- Review each change at four and eight weeks; keep original data exports and prompt receipts.

# Why the upgrade is not a directory or listicle

- The first page is a workflow-selection decision, not “50 AI use cases.”
- The second is a firm-governance and tool-decision framework, not “best AI tools for architects.”
- The third is an operating model and transition hub, not a technology-stack catalog.

Named products may appear in dated companion material when they clarify a decision. The durable pages should organize work, ownership, evidence, and transition decisions—not attempt to out-catalog AI in AEC, AEC Hub, or software publishers.
