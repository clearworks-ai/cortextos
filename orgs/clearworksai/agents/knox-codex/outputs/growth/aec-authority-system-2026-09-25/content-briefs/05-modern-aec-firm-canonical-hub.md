# The Modern AEC Firm canonical hub

## Decision this hub must help a buyer make

**Primary buyer question:** Given our firm’s size and current operating pressure, which capability should we strengthen next, what foundation did we skip, what should we avoid buying too early, and how will we know the change is working?

**Primary readers:** AEC firm owners, principals, operations/finance/technology leaders, emerging team leads, portfolio operators, and practitioners preparing for a size transition.

**Decision outcome:** orient the reader inside one coherent operating model, route them to the right evidence/resource, and make the difference between public guidance, the book, community application, a firm-specific audit, and implementation legible.

## Canonical-role recommendation

Keep `/resources/modern-aec-firm-playbook` as the single organizing hub. Do not create five near-duplicate firm-size pages or seven generic pillar pages merely to target variants. The current research did not verify distinct demand for 50- or 150-person modifiers, and most exact size/stack long-tails were zero reported or unknown.

The hub should be the public map. It should not become the entire book, a software catalog, a maturity test with false precision, or a collection of thin SEO spokes.

## Core intellectual structure

### Seven connected capabilities

1. Leadership & Culture
2. Workflow & Operations
3. Technology & AI
4. Knowledge Transfer
5. Business Model & Finance
6. Positioning & Brand
7. Business Development

### Five operating archetypes

1. Solo 1
2. Small 5
3. Growing 15
4. Established 50
5. Large 150

### How to read the model

- Start with the closest size, recognizing that headcount is only a proxy.
- Read one size backward for a foundation that may have been skipped.
- Read one size forward for the pressure likely to appear next.
- Follow the problem across pillars because operating value usually appears at a handoff.
- Choose one observable change and define owner, evidence, accepted record, measure, and next-scale trigger.

## Claim ledger

### Claims allowed now

- The Modern AEC Firm Playbook is Clearworks’ seven-pillar, five-size operating model.
- Headcount is a planning lens, not a maturity score or a prediction.
- A solo principal, 15-person studio, and 150-person practice may need the same capability but should not build or govern it the same way.
- Workflows, technology, knowledge, finance, leadership, positioning, and business development are connected.
- The public hub can help a firm identify a next question; a firm-specific recommendation requires evidence from that firm.
- A technology decision is also a decision about ownership, information, review, support, business value, and the rest of the operating system.
- The public page provides selected principles and tools; the book contains the full durable synthesis and transition logic.

### Claims not allowed without new evidence

- The seven pillars or five sizes are an industry standard.
- Every architecture firm follows the same progression.
- A total score defines firm maturity or predicts profitability, growth, AI readiness, or project outcomes.
- The size bands reflect measured prevalence or statistically representative benchmarks.
- The hub ranks for its navigational phrase, owns “Modern AEC Firm,” or appears alongside another site on Google/Bing.
- A size-specific software stack is universally correct.
- Clearworks benchmark data by size until the collection method, sample, and permission model support publication.
- A download, anonymous session, or AI referral caused a later opportunity without a valid join and CRM evidence.

## Proposed direct answer

> A modern AEC firm does not require every leader to rebuild the business from memory. Priorities, project state, knowledge, money, systems, proof, and relationships become visible enough to hand off and improve. What “modern” requires changes with the firm’s size, disciplines, offices, project types, and client obligations. The next move is the weakest connection the firm can make observable and own—not the newest software purchase.

## Modern AEC Firm Benchmark: original-data plan

The benchmark should become the hub’s public evidence product, but only after the collection and consent design exists.

### Candidate questions

- Which recurring workflows consume the most reported staff time?
- Where does current state disappear or require manual reconstruction?
- Which decisions still depend on one person?
- Which systems have named owners and accepted records?
- Which AI experiments reached sustained live use?
- What are the most common ownership, permission, source, and review gaps?
- What changes first at Solo, 5, 15, 50, and 150-person stages?
- Which choices were configure, buy, connect, build, defer, or leave alone?

### Minimum methodological requirements

- Define population, collection period, inclusion criteria, and unit of analysis.
- Separate respondents, firms, interviews, workflows, and artifacts.
- Prevent one firm with many interviews from inflating prevalence.
- Track named, anonymized, composite, and synthesis-only permission boundaries.
- Publish sample size beside every percentage.
- Label staff estimates, observations, modeled opportunities, implementation events, and realized outcomes separately.
- Do not release firm-size comparisons with cells too small for privacy or stability.
- Do not call a convenience sample representative.
- Publish methodology, revisions, and source limitations.

## Why this is not a directory or listicle

The hub does not catalog AI tools or rank providers. It organizes the operating decisions that change as an AEC firm grows and links each decision to evidence, ownership, and a next action. Dated companion material may compare tools or prices, but the canonical hub remains a durable operating model. This is the strategic distinction from directory-scale competitors: they catalog the market; the Modern AEC Firm hub explains how a firm’s operating system must change.
