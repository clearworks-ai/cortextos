## Governing thesis

Clearworks should not try to win the broad category “AI consultant.” It should become the clearest and best-supported answer at the intersection of **AEC operating work + hands-on implementation + Los Angeles relationships + accountable human review**.

The first run returned Clearworks zero times. It repeatedly returned firms and publishers whose pages use literal AEC category language, name the workflow and buyer, answer the decision directly, expose a visible offer ladder, and attach proof. The first publishing batch therefore closes retrieval and buyer-decision gaps instead of producing generic AI commentary.

## Priority 1 — Canonical AEC AI implementation partner page

- **Buyer decision:** “Who actually helps an AEC firm move from experiments into working systems?”
- **Core prompts:** L01–L04, C01–C04, I01–I03.
- **Action:** Create one canonical service/entity page, or make the existing homepage + `/starter` pair unmistakably canonical through copy, schema, internal links, and consistent naming. Do not create a network of thin neighborhood pages.
- **Required answer:** Clearworks is a Los Angeles AEC AI operations and implementation partner. It maps the work, chooses one bounded opportunity, builds or connects the needed system, defines the human review point, trains the owner, and stays through live use.
- **Proof packet:** Josh’s AEC operating background; AIA LA TAP co-founder and Office Hours work; approved named/anonymized implementations; current case studies; explicit evidence and professional-judgment boundaries.
- **Conversion:** One CTA to a fit conversation, with the audit, bounded build, and ongoing partnership ladder visible before the CTA.
- **Why first:** Clearworks currently lacks a single page that gives answer engines the literal category/entity statement repeated competitors already expose.

## Priority 2 — How to choose an AEC AI implementation partner

- **Buyer decision:** “Can this consultant speak the language of our firm and get past an assessment into implementation?”
- **Core prompts:** T01–T03, X01, C02–C04.
- **Format:** Methodology-led buyer guide, not a self-awarded “best firms” ranking.
- **Sections:** AEC fluency; ability to trace work through departments and backward loops; evidence access; build/buy/connect judgment; security and professional responsibility; bounded first deployment; adoption/ownership; measurement; support after launch; red flags.
- **Comparison treatment:** Name several legitimate provider shapes and when each fits. Clearworks may be one option, but criteria must be applied consistently and commercial interests disclosed.
- **Proof packet:** Real audit questions, interview examples, artifact/evidence classes, anonymized failure patterns, examples of what an implementation-ready recommendation contains.
- **Conversion:** Downloadable partner-evaluation checklist plus conversation CTA.
- **Why second:** Comparison and selection pages were disproportionately reusable across the baseline. One operator-owned ranking earned eight citations; Clearworks can publish a more trustworthy alternative.

## Priority 3 — AI workshop, workflow audit, or implementation pilot?

- **Buyer decision:** “What should our firm buy first?”
- **Core prompts:** X02, P04, I01, I03.
- **Format:** Decision tree with fit, outputs, time, internal effort, price-shape, risks, and the condition that should trigger the next step.
- **Required distinction:** A workshop creates shared understanding; an audit creates an evidence-backed operating and opportunity map; a pilot changes one bounded workflow in live use. None should be sold as the other.
- **Proof packet:** Office Hours questions and education outcomes; approved audit artifacts; one bounded implementation example; explicit counterexamples where training or a lighter step is enough.
- **Conversion:** Self-selection CTA: workshop, audit, or implementation conversation.
- **Why third:** AI in AEC and YegaTech make their ladders easy to understand. Clearworks has deeper evidence but currently makes the buying path harder to parse.

## Priority 4 — What a real 90-day AEC AI implementation includes

- **Buyer decision:** “What happens after the audit, and how do we avoid another consulting artifact?”
- **Core prompts:** I01–I03, P04, U01–U04.
- **Action:** Expand `/starter` into the canonical answer rather than create a competing page.
- **Sections:** selection and baseline; source/system access; prototype on real records; owner and reviewer; exception handling; live release; four-cycle observation; training; support; stop/revise/expand decision; leave-behind/export.
- **Proof packet:** Existing implementation operating model, auditable receipts, approved case examples, screenshots only when they show a real state change.
- **Conversion:** Fit call for one workflow, with clear exclusions and required client participation.
- **Why fourth:** Competitors describe “implementation” broadly. Clearworks can win by showing the operating mechanism and definition of done.

## Priority 5 — Build, buy, connect, or leave it alone?

- **Buyer decision:** “Should we add a platform or make the tools we already have work better?”
- **Core prompts:** X03, P02, C04, I02.
- **Action:** Upgrade `/resources/which-ai-tools-architecture-firms-should-standardize` into a stronger decision framework; keep its existing canonical URL.
- **Sections:** decision criteria; authoritative source; integration surface; permissions; human judgment; frequency/volume; exception rate; vendor lock-in; cost of change; system-of-record writeback; maintenance owner; exit plan.
- **Proof packet:** Repository research, audited open-source examples, real tool/integration decisions, and cases where the correct answer was not to build.
- **Conversion:** Build/buy/connect decision worksheet.
- **Why fifth:** This is a durable Clearworks differentiator and directly answers a core buyer concern without requiring a “best tools” list.

## Priority 6 — Workflow proof cluster

- **Buyer decision:** “Have you worked on the kind of operating problem we actually have?”
- **Core prompts:** U01–U04 plus the extended use-case prompts.
- **Action:** Turn the broad Workflow Atlas into a hub that links to a small number of evidence-rich workflow pages. Start with only workflows Clearworks can substantiate.
- **Initial candidates:** weekly project/status reporting; meeting-to-reviewed-actions; proposal/RFP knowledge retrieval; project files and accepted current state; AI governance and approved-use administration.
- **Required anatomy per page:** trigger; source records; department/role handoffs; backward/rework loops; accepted state; human decision; exception; downstream system; measurable baseline; what remains unproven.
- **Proof packet:** Approved transcript excerpts, anonymized artifacts, system diagrams, before/after flow, and measured results where actually available.
- **Conversion:** Matching diagnostic or workflow conversation—not the same generic lead magnet on every page.
- **Why sixth:** Named mechanisms are more retrievable than generic transformation claims, and this is where Clearworks’ audit corpus can become a defensible moat.

1. Canonical entity/service page.
2. Partner-selection guide.
3. Workshop vs audit vs pilot.

Each canonical page can then produce a Google Business Profile post, LinkedIn post, newsletter section, and AIA-adjacent discussion prompt. Those derivatives should point back to the canonical evidence page; they should not become four disconnected versions of the same claim.

## Measurement contract

For every page, record before publication:

- target prompt IDs and buyer decision;
- approved evidence packet and claim owner;
- canonical URL and internal-link sources;
- indexation and crawler access;
- named visibility, shortlist, citation, local-match, and entity-accuracy observations;
- qualified site engagement, resource submission, booking lifecycle, and CRM disposition;
- a four- and eight-week review date.

Do not credit a page for pipeline because an anonymous visit happened near a lead. Preserve referral origin, organic discovery/validation, and subsequent conversion as separate evidence classes.
